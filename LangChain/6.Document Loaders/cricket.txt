Underneath a summer sky of endless, open blue,

Upon a field of cut-green velvet, morning drinks the dew.

Two wooden sticks mark out the ground, twenty-two yards long,

Where legends rise and heartbreaks fall, and silence meets a song.

The coin flies high, a silver flash, to decide the fate and day,

And twenty-two brave souls step out to join the sacred play.

The leather ball, a polished sphere, painted cherry-red,

Holds whispers of the swing and seam, of where the pitch has led.

The bowler stands at mark's retreat, a deep breath fills his chest,

A silent vow to test his foe, to give his absolute best.

He charges in with rhythm built, a storm about to break,

Each pounding stride upon the turf makes dormant spirits wake.

At the crease, the batsman waits, motionless and still,

A stoic mind, a wooden blade, a quiet, stubborn will.

He taps the crease—*one, two, three*—and adjusts his weighted glove,

Engaged in this ancient dance of battle and of love.

The arm whips through! The ball takes flight, a streak of sudden pace,

It nips across the hardened pitch, a challenge to the face.

A flash of willow cuts the air, a sharp and wooden *CRACK!*

The ball shoots through the cover gap, leaving no turning back.

The crowd erupts, a roaring wave, as boundary ropes are found,

And four quick runs are added to the scoreboard on the ground.

### The Midday Grind

Hours pass beneath the sun, the game begins to turn,

In Test match deep or T20, the fires continue to burn.

The spin bowling wizard steps up now, with flight and clever deceit,

Drifting the ball like a phantom's breath to make the batsman cheat.

It grips, it turns, it takes the edge! A razor-thin mistake!

The keeper dives with outstretched hands—a sudden, breathless take!

An umpire’s finger raises high into the dusty air,

A moment caught in frozen time, a drama sweet and fair.

The walking bat heads to the room, head bowed in quiet grace,

While fielders leap in wild embrace, a frenzy takes the place.

---

### The Heartbeat of the Game

For cricket is a paradox, both delicate and tough,

A game of polite, quiet gestures, yet physically so rough.

It demands the patience of a saint, the reflex of a cat,

The courage to stand up again when struck upon the hat.

It’s tea and biscuits on the grass, it’s floodlights in the dark,

It’s children playing street-side games with plastic in the park.

From Melbourne to Lord’s grassy hill, from Mumbai to West Indies shores,

It speaks a universal tongue that opens locked-up doors.

When evening falls and shadows stretch across the fading green,

The players shake their weary hands upon the quiet scene.

No matter who has won or lost, when all is said and done,

They bound themselves to history beneath the setting sun.

For long after the stumps are pulled and crowds have walked away,

The ghost-like echoes of the game will forever choose to stay.
